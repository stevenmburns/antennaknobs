  every knob's bounds and caption; the hand-written panel per engine is
  gone. A control appears exactly when the engine takes it, a refused
  combination greys out with momwire's own sentence, and a design a tab
  cannot solve — a buried deck under the extended kernel — says so inline
  before you press anything. The feed-model choice now reaches the B-spline

  `surface` convention: radials in the grass, served as the elevated family
  at the jacket's outer radius — where an insulated wire's conductor
  actually sits — with momwire's advisory when the stand-off is small enough
  that the height is the answer
  ([momwire#872](https://github.com/stevenmburns/momwire/issues/872),
  [#875](https://github.com/stevenmburns/momwire/issues/875)). The coated

## The whole pitch, in one line

antennaknobs describes an antenna **as Python code** — a small parametric
*builder*, its geometry computed from named knobs — then lets you explore it two
ways: drive it from a script (sweep, optimize, compare, export a NEC deck), or
open it in a browser and **turn the knobs live**, watching the radiation
pattern, SWR, and impedance respond as you drag. Most antenna tools are a static
formula calculator or a desktop app you have to install; this is neither.
