<Card title="New in v0.121.0" icon="star">
  **OpenNEC on the hosted app.** The [simulator](https://app.antennaknobs.dev)
  now has the **OpenNEC** tab beside PyNEC, with the same size and time limits.
  What OpenNEC cannot solve is refused by name before it reaches the engine: a
  frequency out of range, a structure thousands of wavelengths across, or a
  wire too short to mesh. An answer that comes back not a number is refused
  too, instead of being shown.

  **On momwire 0.93.0.** The momwire command-line launchers are faster on
  NEC-2 decks with many frequencies. The razor-2p executable fills a buried
  structure in less memory, holding one whole matrix where it held two: a hub
  of 32 buried radials drops from 4.0 GB to 1.5 GB.

  **Also in v0.120.2:**
  **A wire too short to mesh is refused by name.** A design or opened deck
  whose wire segments came out zero, vanishingly short or not a number, even
  with the wire's two ends apart, could hang the PyNEC engine or stop the
  OpenNEC engine outright, and gave a meaningless answer elsewhere. Every
  engine now refuses such a wire by name before solving, naming the wire, its
  segment count and the length it would have had. Real designs are untouched:
  the floor is a nanometre, and the shortest segment in the catalog is 3 mm.

  **Also in v0.120.1:**
  **End-fed designs over real ground now give the right impedance.** On the
  B-spline engine, an end feed (a `PortAtEnd`) on a design where any wire
  crosses the ground surface, such as a ground stake or a radial that dips into
  the soil, gave a wildly wrong impedance (hundreds of thousands of ohms
  reactive) without any warning. momwire 0.92.0 fixes it, and this release
  picks that up. Designs without a wire through the ground surface are
  unchanged.
</Card>

## The whole pitch, in one line

antennaknobs describes an antenna **as Python code** — a small parametric
*builder*, its geometry computed from named knobs — then lets you explore it two
ways: drive it from a script (sweep, optimize, compare, export a NEC deck), or
open it in a browser and **turn the knobs live**, watching the radiation
pattern, SWR, and impedance respond as you drag. Most antenna tools are a static
formula calculator or a desktop app you have to install; this is neither.
