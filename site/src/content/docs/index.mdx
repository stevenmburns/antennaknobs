  **The ceiling we told you about last release is gone.** v0.65.0 said

  physics settled. momwire 0.46.0 removed that limit

  simulator's control now goes to **32**.

  **What that was costing.** On a buried radial vertical, the default of 8
  sits **3.1 Ω from the converged answer** — essentially all of it
  reactance, with resistance pinned at 75.85 Ω while X runs 43.6 Ω down to
  40.5 Ω. Turning the knob to 32 closes that to 0.3 Ω. If you model ground
  systems, this is the release where that error becomes something you can
  actually dial out.

  **And it is nearly free now.** A separate fix stopped the buried fill
  from leaving the accelerator on every below-surface pair, so the whole
  ladder from 4 to 32 costs about the same as it used to cost at 8.

  **The knob is honest about itself, too.** It matters on decks with wire
  at or below the ground and does almost nothing on decks standing clear —
  0.02 Ω between 8 and 32 on an elevated antenna. The tooltip now says so,
  because trying it on a dipole, seeing nothing move, and concluding the
  control is broken was a reasonable thing to do.

  **Razor joins the engine picker**, as `razor-2p` — the tent basis tested
  by NEC-5's razor-blade rule, offered as the two-point lane only.

  **We also attacked our own published result.** The
  [buried radials](/advanced/buried-radials/) case study's central finding
  — a disagreement that *grows* with radial count — was measured at the
  old quadrature, and the error we had just characterised had the same
  shape. Re-run at converged quadrature, it held: the quadrature part is
  common-mode, the same with four buried radials as with none. The page

## The whole pitch, in one line

antennaknobs describes an antenna **as Python code** — a small parametric
*builder*, its geometry computed from named knobs — then lets you explore it two
ways: drive it from a script (sweep, optimize, compare, export a NEC deck), or
open it in a browser and **turn the knobs live**, watching the radiation
pattern, SWR, and impedance respond as you drag. Most antenna tools are a static
formula calculator or a desktop app you have to install; this is neither.
