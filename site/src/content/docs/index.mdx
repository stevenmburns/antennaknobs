  **On momwire 0.98.0, with two speed switches.** A momwire tab's gear menu
  has **reuse repeated geometry (faster fill)** on the `bs2`, Sinusoidal and

  **Yagis on one element core.** The `beams.yagi` design takes a
  `uniform_cores` option that builds every element on the director's core,
  so the pairs between elements repeat. On the 10-element Yagi, Sinusoidal's
  fill reuse then compresses the fill 46× instead of 5.4×, and the impedance
  moves by 0.02 Ω.

  **More for writing geometry.** `TransformStack` gains `st.path` (a run of
  wires that share their corners exactly), the axis wires `wire_x`, `wire_y`
  and `wire_z`, and `Transform.mirrorYZ` / `mirrorXZ` / `mirrorXY`. See the
  [reference](/reference/drone-transform/).

  **Insulated wires on the ground reopen as you wrote them.** A NEC-5 deck
  exported from a design with insulated radials lying on the soil now opens
  with each jacket resting on the ground, so bs2 solves it instead of
  refusing it as sunk into the soil.

  settings, kept studies and on the command line, and are read as the new
  ones. `razor-nec5` is retired. A ↺ button beside the design picker puts
  every knob back on the design's own values.

## The whole pitch, in one line

antennaknobs describes an antenna **as Python code** — a small parametric
*builder*, its geometry computed from named knobs — then lets you explore it two
ways: drive it from a script (sweep, optimize, compare, export a NEC deck), or
open it in a browser and **turn the knobs live**, watching the radiation
pattern, SWR, and impedance respond as you drag. Most antenna tools are a static
formula calculator or a desktop app you have to install; this is neither.
