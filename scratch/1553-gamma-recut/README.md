Each study's own README carries its dated "ΔΓ re-cut" section, generated by

## The headline: on a bounded metric, one verdict changes and one premise was wrong

| design | verdict on relative Z | verdict on ΔΓ | ΔΓ at served rung | ΔΓ fall 12→30 | reference's own ΔΓ |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `dipoles.short_dipole_loaded` | reference | **density (changed)** | 0.9001 | 50 % | 0.0536 |
| `specialty.continuous_helix` | density | density | 0.2195 | 78 % | 0.0026 |
| `wire.zepp` | placement | placement | 0.1657 | −1 % | 0.0118 |

**`dipoles.short_dipole_loaded` flips from *reference* to *density*, and the
relative-Z metric is why it was misclassified.** On relative Z the reference
appeared to move 12.37 % of `|Z_ref|` between ×160 and ×320, which disqualified it
from arbitrating the row. But that 12.37 % was inflated by the same small
denominator inflating everything else on this design. On ΔΓ the reference's own
uncertainty is **0.0536** against a served-rung error of **0.9001** — the
reference is **17× better than the row it is judging** and arbitrates it easily.

## The premise this re-cut was given is wrong, and it matters

The brief says short_dipole_loaded's 240 % "is ~1.3 Ω of X at Z ≈ 13 + j13
(ΔΓ ≈ 0.03)". Those numbers are real but they belong to a **different row**:

| comparison | \|ΔZ\| | ΔΓ |
|---|---:|---:|
| razor@160 vs bs2@160 (the *arbitration*) | 1.34 Ω | **0.0323** |
| NEC-5@160 vs bs2@160 (the *arbitration*) | 1.28 Ω | 0.0309 |
| **bs3@12 vs bs2@160 (the served *tail row*)** | **40.60 Ω** | **0.9001** |

1.3 Ω and ΔΓ 0.03 is how far the *oracles* sit from the reference. The tail row
the issue is about is 40.6 Ω and ΔΓ 0.90. Conflating them is what made the tail
look like an artefact.

**So the 240 % was NOT a metric artefact**, and the part of my own AK#1552 write-up
that said the tail figures were "not a defect in d=2 or d=3 at their served
densities" is **wrong and is corrected here.** At d=3 N=12 this design's reactance
comes out at **−28.14j** where the converged answer is **+12.44j** — the sign is
wrong, `|Z|` is 31.6 against 18.0, and Γ moves most of the way across the chart.
A user at the served density would see a completely different match.

What survives from AK#1552 is the *mechanism*: the +j818 Ω centre load against the
dipole's capacitive X leaves a ~17 Ω residual, so a ~5 % error in the antenna's own
X becomes a 40 Ω error in the driving point. **Conditioning explains why d=3 at 12
is so far off on this design. It does not excuse it**, and ΔΓ is what makes that
distinction visible.

The medians rank exactly as they did on relative Z. **The tails do not**: d=3 at
12's worst is 0.90 where d=1 at 20's is 0.10, and on a [0, 2] scale 0.90 is not a
rounding artefact. For context, the July corpus benchmark's own headline for
BSpline d=2 against nec2c was a median ΔΓ of 0.0408 — an order worse than any
served rung here, so all three densities are good in the median by that standard.

would do, in the direction of making a real error look like an artefact rather than
the reverse.

## What this changes for the density table

Nothing is proposed here; `density.py` is untouched. But the AK#1552 conclusion it
would have rested on is now reversed on this point: **there is a density-grounds
case to answer for d=3 at 12**, on `dipoles.short_dipole_loaded` at least, and
saying so is the reason this re-cut was worth doing.
