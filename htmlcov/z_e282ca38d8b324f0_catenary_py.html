1"""Hanging-chain equilibrium: the shape a real wire takes under its own weight. 

2 

3Every catalog wire in a design is a perfectly straight line, but a wire strung 

4between an apex and a rigging point hangs in a catenary set by its weight per 

5metre and how it is tensioned. This module is the pure-geometry half of issue 

6#698: given a serial chain of segments — each with a fixed arc length and a 

7weight per metre — and one of two rig closures, it returns the chain's actual 

8shape in its own vertical plane. 

9 

10Nothing here imports engines, the builder, the network spec, or the web layer: 

11it is 2-D statics and closed forms only. The design layer (a later unit) 

12embeds the returned plane curve into 3-D at some azimuth and emits it as a 

13chord polyline. 

14 

15Coordinates and sign conventions 

16-------------------------------- 

17Work in the chain's own vertical plane: ``x`` runs horizontally in the 

18direction the chain travels, ``z`` is up. Gravity is ``-z``. 

19 

20The internal tension state at arc position ``s`` is the vector 

21 

22 t(s) = (H, V(s)) 

23 

24meaning *the force the downstream material exerts on the upstream material* — 

25it pulls forward, along increasing arc length. Statics on an element gives 

26``dt/ds = (0, +w)``, so 

27 

28 * ``H`` — the horizontal component — is **constant along the whole chain**, 

29 and is the single scalar that sets the catenary's shape; and 

30 * ``V(s) = V0 + w·s`` accumulates the weight hung below. 

31 

32``H > 0`` always, which also makes ``x`` strictly increasing along the chain 

33(``dx/ds = H/T > 0``). A chain hanging straight down has ``H = 0``: a real 

34equilibrium, but a degenerate one this formulation cannot express, so the 

35solvers reject it with an explicit error rather than limping toward it. 

36 

37The *support* forces reported on :class:`ChainSolution` follow from the same 

38convention: the apex support must supply ``-t(0)`` and the far support ``+t(L)`` 

39so that 

40 

41 F_apex + F_end = (0, Σ Lᵢ·wᵢ) 

42 

43— horizontal components cancel exactly, verticals sum to the total weight. 

44That identity is the module's cheapest global correctness check and is 

45asserted in the tests. 

46 

47Closed forms 

48------------ 

49With ``a = H/w`` the catenary parameter and ``v = V/H`` the local slope: 

50 

51 z(x) = a·cosh((x − x_v)/a) + c s(x) = a·sinh((x − x_v)/a) 

52 T(x) = H·cosh((x − x_v)/a) = w·(z(x) − c) = √(H² + V²) 

53 

54Marching a segment means: from the start position and ``(H, V0)`` advance the 

55arc length ``L`` and read off the end position and ``V1 = V0 + w·L``. The 

56displacement over an arc length ``s`` is, exactly, 

57 

58 Δx = a·(asinh(v₁) − asinh(v₀)) Δz = (T₁ − T₀)/w 

59 

60Note that ``Δz`` never evaluates ``cosh`` — the naive ``a·cosh(x/a)`` form 

61overflows long before a taut wire is taut enough to matter. See "Numerics". 

62 

63Architecture: a swappable marching kernel 

64----------------------------------------- 

65The solve is split into 

66 

67 1. a **marching kernel** (:func:`march_continuous`) that turns 

68 ``(start, (H, V0), segments)`` into per-segment polylines and end states, 

69 and 

70 2. a **2-unknown shooting wrapper** that hunts for the ``(H, V0)`` satisfying 

71 the rig closure. 

72 

73Only the continuous closed-form kernel ships now. Keeping it behind the 

74:class:`MarchKernel` call signature is deliberate: a *discrete funicular* 

75kernel (per-node force balance) slots in behind exactly the same interface 

76when point masses — insulators, a hanging feedline — or per-segment wind/ice 

77loads are wanted, and those break the closed forms but not the shooting 

78wrapper. The continuous kernel ships first because it makes the mechanical 

79shape exact and independent of chord count: a convergence ladder then refines 

80only the *electrical* discretization, never a moving geometric target (the 

81#606 lesson — do not let a discretization parameter read as physics). 

82 

83Deliberately out of scope, stated honestly: 

84 

85 * **Wire elasticity.** Copper stretch is second-order at these tensions and 

86 an inextensible chain stays closed-form. A stretching chain couples the 

87 arc length to the tension and turns every march into an inner solve. 

88 * **Wind and ice loading.** This is the follow-up the kernel seam exists 

89 for: a transverse distributed load is not a vertical-plane catenary. 

90 * **Ground contact of a slack wire.** A wire that touches the ground needs 

91 a constrained energy minimization; the catenary simply cannot express the 

92 unilateral contact condition, and pretending otherwise would silently 

93 return a shape that passes below grade. 

94 * **Non-serial topologies** — branches, sliding pulleys, multi-wire 

95 halyards. Those need the fully assembled per-node force-balance system, 

96 which is not worth building until such a design actually appears. 

97 

98Numerics: the taut limit 

99------------------------ 

100As a wire is pulled taut, ``a = H/w`` grows without bound and every naive form 

101falls over. The governing small parameter is 

102 

103 d = w·L/H = L/a 

104 

105— the segment's arc length measured in catenary parameters, i.e. how much the 

106chain bends over the segment. For ``d`` small the exact displacement formulas 

107subtract two nearly equal numbers of size ``a``, so their *absolute* error is 

108``eps·a``: at ``a = 10¹⁰ m`` that is microns of garbage in a millimetre-scale 

109answer. Below :data:`TAUT_D` the module therefore switches to a midpoint 

110Taylor expansion in ``d`` (the small-sag / parabolic expansion, carried to 

111``d⁴``), which is cancellation-free and reduces to a straight line at ``d = 0`` 

112— so massless segments (``w = 0``, a taut halyard) fall out of the same code 

113path with no special case. The threshold is chosen where the two branches 

114agree to ~1e-13 relative; :func:`chord_sag` uses a second, tighter threshold 

115because a perpendicular sag is a far more cancellation-sensitive quantity than 

116a position. Both are exercised by continuity tests that straddle them. 

117 

118Issue #698.

129# Standard gravity, m/s². Catalog wire weights are grams per metre; the chain 

130# math wants newtons per metre. 

131GRAVITY = 9.80665 

132 

133# Switch to the midpoint series when d = w·L/H falls below this. Rationale: 

134# at d = 1e-3 the exact form's cancellation error is ~eps/d ≈ 2e-13 relative 

135# while the series truncates at O(d⁴/1920) ≈ 5e-16 relative, so the two agree 

136# to ~2e-13 and the crossover is seamless. Larger d and the series would 

137# truncate visibly; smaller and the exact form would already be losing digits. 

138TAUT_D = 1.0e-3 

139 

140# The same crossover for the chord-perpendicular sag, which is a *difference* 

141# of O(L²) products divided by O(L) — its absolute error is ~eps·L against a 

142# sag of ~L·d/8, i.e. a relative error of ~8·eps/d. Matching that against the 

143# transverse-string formula's O(d²) truncation puts the balance point two 

144# decades lower than TAUT_D: at d = 1e-5 both branches carry ~1e-10 relative 

145# error. A single shared threshold would leave a visible 1e-6 step in the sag. 

146TAUT_SAG_D = 1.0e-5 

147 

148# Newton's residuals are non-dimensionalised (positions by the chain's total 

149# length, forces by whichever of the rope pull and the chain's own weight is 

150# larger), so one tolerance covers both closures at any scale. 

151_NEWTON_TOL = 1.0e-11 

152_NEWTON_MAX_ITER = 80

227def _offset(h: float, v0: float, w: float, s: float) -> tuple[float, float]: 

228 """Displacement ``(Δx, Δz)`` after advancing arc length ``s``. 

229 

230 Exact catenary displacement, written to avoid the two ways the textbook 

231 form dies in the taut limit: ``Δz`` is ``(T₁ − T₀)/w`` rather than a 

232 difference of ``a·cosh`` (no overflow), and below :data:`TAUT_D` both 

233 components come from a midpoint Taylor expansion in ``d = w·s/h`` (no 

234 cancellation). ``w = 0`` lands in the series branch with ``d = 0`` and 

235 yields the straight-line result exactly. 

236 """ 

237 if s == 0.0: 

238 return 0.0, 0.0 

239 slope0 = v0 / h 

240 d = w * s / h 

241 slope1 = slope0 + d 

242 if abs(d) < TAUT_D: 

243 # Midpoint expansion kills the even-order derivatives, so the leading 

244 # correction is O(d²) and the next is O(d⁴): 

245 # Δx = s·[f'(m) + f'''(m)·d²/24 + f⁽⁵⁾(m)·d⁴/1920], f = asinh 

246 # Δz = s·[g'(m) + g'''(m)·d²/24 + g⁽⁵⁾(m)·d⁴/1920], g = √(1+v²) 

247 m = 0.5 * (slope0 + slope1) 

248 m2 = m * m 

249 g = math.sqrt(1.0 + m2) 

250 g5 = g**5 

251 g9 = g**9 

252 d2 = d * d 

253 d4 = d2 * d2 

254 fx = ( 

255 1.0 / g 

256 + (2.0 * m2 - 1.0) / g5 * (d2 / 24.0) 

257 + (9.0 - 72.0 * m2 + 24.0 * m2 * m2) / g9 * (d4 / 1920.0) 

258 ) 

259 fz = ( 

260 m / g 

261 + (-3.0 * m) / g5 * (d2 / 24.0) 

262 + (45.0 * m - 60.0 * m2 * m) / g9 * (d4 / 1920.0) 

263 ) 

264 return s * fx, s * fz 

265 a = h / w 

266 dx = a * (math.asinh(slope1) - math.asinh(slope0)) 

267 dz = (math.hypot(h, v0 + w * s) - math.hypot(h, v0)) / w 

268 return dx, dz

271def chord_sag(h: float, v0: float, w: float, length_m: float) -> float: 

272 """Max distance of a catenary segment from its own chord, measured ⟂. 

273 

274 A catenary is convex (``z'' = cosh(·)/a > 0``), so the arc lies entirely 

275 below its chord and the maximum offset is unsigned and unambiguous. It 

276 occurs where the tangent is parallel to the chord, i.e. where the local 

277 slope ``V/H`` equals the chord slope — the mean value theorem guarantees 

278 such a point exists because ``x`` is monotone. 

279 

280 Below :data:`TAUT_SAG_D` the exact cross-product form has cancelled away 

281 its significant digits, so the sag comes from the taut-string result 

282 ``q·C²/(8·T)`` with the transverse load ``q = w·Δx/C``: 

283 

284 sag ≈ w·Δx·C/(8·T_mid) 

285 

286 which reduces to the familiar ``w·d²/(8H)`` for a level span. 

287 """ 

288 if w == 0.0: 

289 return 0.0 

290 dx, dz = _offset(h, v0, w, length_m) 

291 chord = math.hypot(dx, dz) 

292 if chord == 0.0: 

293 return 0.0 

294 if w * length_m / h < TAUT_SAG_D: 

295 t_mid = math.hypot(h, v0 + 0.5 * w * length_m) 

296 return w * dx * chord / (8.0 * t_mid) 

297 # Tangent parallel to the chord: V* = H·(Δz/Δx), reached at arc length 

298 # s* = (V* − V0)/w. Its perpendicular offset is the 2-D cross product 

299 # with the unit chord. 

300 v_star = h * (dz / dx) 

301 s_star = (v_star - v0) / w 

302 s_star = min(max(s_star, 0.0), length_m) 

303 px, pz = _offset(h, v0, w, s_star) 

304 return abs((dx * pz - dz * px) / chord)

307def march_continuous( 

308 start: tuple[float, float], 

309 tension: tuple[float, float], 

310 segments: Sequence[ChainSegment], 

311 samples_per_segment: int = 33, 

312) -> tuple[SegmentMarch, ...]: 

313 """March a serial chain with the continuous closed-form catenary. 

314 

315 Given the start point, the forward internal tension ``(H, V0)`` there, and 

316 the segment list, walk segment by segment: each one is an exact catenary 

317 arc of its own parameter ``a = H/wᵢ`` (a straight line where ``wᵢ = 0``), 

318 joined to the next at the position and tension the previous one ended at. 

319 ``H`` is shared by every segment — that is what makes the chain serial and 

320 the whole problem two-unknown. 

321 

322 Raises :class:`ValueError` for ``H ≤ 0`` (a vertical hanging chain; see the 

323 module docstring) or a sample count below 2. 

324 """ 

325 h, v = tension 

326 if not (h > 0.0): 

327 raise ValueError( 

328 f"horizontal tension must be positive, got H = {h!r} N; a chain " 

329 "hanging straight down (H = 0) is a degenerate equilibrium this " 

330 "catenary formulation cannot represent" 

331 ) 

332 if not math.isfinite(h) or not math.isfinite(v): 

333 raise ValueError(f"non-finite tension state (H, V0) = ({h!r}, {v!r})") 

334 if samples_per_segment < 2: 

335 raise ValueError( 

336 f"samples_per_segment must be at least 2 (both endpoints), got " 

337 f"{samples_per_segment}" 

338 ) 

339 if not segments: 

340 raise ValueError("a chain needs at least one segment") 

341 

342 x, z = start 

343 out: list[SegmentMarch] = [] 

344 for seg in segments: 

345 length = seg.length_m 

346 w = seg.weight_n_per_m 

347 pts = np.empty((samples_per_segment, 2), dtype=float) 

348 for i in range(samples_per_segment): 

349 s = length * i / (samples_per_segment - 1) 

350 dx, dz = _offset(h, v, w, s) 

351 pts[i, 0] = x + dx 

352 pts[i, 1] = z + dz 

353 # Recompute the endpoint from the closed form rather than trusting the 

354 # last sample's rounding, then hand the exact state to the next span. 

355 dx_end, dz_end = _offset(h, v, w, length) 

356 x, z = x + dx_end, z + dz_end 

357 pts[-1, 0] = x 

358 pts[-1, 1] = z 

359 v = v + w * length 

360 out.append(SegmentMarch(points=pts, end_point=(x, z), end_tension=(h, v))) 

361 return tuple(out)

396@dataclass(frozen=True, eq=False) 

397class ChainSolution: 

398 """A solved hanging chain in its own vertical plane. 

399 

400 ``segments`` carries the sampled shape and per-segment diagnostics in chain 

401 order; ``junctions`` are the interior joins between consecutive segments 

402 (empty for a single-segment chain). 

403 

404 The two tension *vectors* are the forces the **supports exert on the 

405 chain**, not the internal tension in a fixed direction — so they sum to the 

406 chain's total weight pointing up and their horizontal parts cancel (see the 

407 module docstring). ``apex_tension`` / ``end_tension`` are their magnitudes, 

408 which are also the internal line tensions at those points. 

409 ``horizontal_tension_n`` is the shared ``H``, the one number that sets the 

410 shape. 

411 """ 

412 

413 apex: tuple[float, float] 

414 end_point: tuple[float, float] 

415 segments: tuple[SegmentShape, ...] 

416 junctions: tuple[tuple[float, float], ...] 

417 apex_tension_vector: tuple[float, float] 

418 end_tension_vector: tuple[float, float] 

419 apex_tension: float 

420 end_tension: float 

421 horizontal_tension_n: float 

422 total_weight_n: float 

423 iterations: int 

424 residual: float 

425 

426 @property 

427 def polyline(self) -> np.ndarray: 

428 """All segments concatenated, duplicate junction samples dropped.""" 

429 parts = [self.segments[0].points] 

430 parts.extend(seg.points[1:] for seg in self.segments[1:]) 

431 return np.vstack(parts) 

432 

433 @property 

434 def max_sag_m(self) -> float: 

435 """Largest per-segment chord sag over the chain.""" 

436 return max(seg.max_sag_m for seg in self.segments) 

437 

438 @property 

439 def total_arc_length_m(self) -> float: 

440 return sum(seg.arc_length_m for seg in self.segments)

443def _assemble( 

444 apex: tuple[float, float], 

445 tension: tuple[float, float], 

446 segments: Sequence[ChainSegment], 

447 marched: Sequence[SegmentMarch], 

448 iterations: int, 

449 residual: float, 

450) -> ChainSolution: 

451 """Package a converged march into a :class:`ChainSolution`.""" 

452 h = tension[0] 

453 v = tension[1] 

454 shapes: list[SegmentShape] = [] 

455 for seg, march in zip(segments, marched): 

456 steps = np.diff(march.points, axis=0) 

457 polyline_length = float(np.hypot(steps[:, 0], steps[:, 1]).sum()) 

458 shapes.append( 

459 SegmentShape( 

460 name=seg.name, 

461 points=march.points, 

462 arc_length_m=seg.length_m, 

463 polyline_length_m=polyline_length, 

464 max_sag_m=chord_sag(h, v, seg.weight_n_per_m, seg.length_m), 

465 weight_n_per_m=seg.weight_n_per_m, 

466 start_tension_vector=(h, v), 

467 end_tension_vector=march.end_tension, 

468 ) 

469 ) 

470 v = march.end_tension[1] 

471 

472 total_weight = sum(s.length_m * s.weight_n_per_m for s in segments) 

473 # Support forces: the apex holds the chain back (−t(0)); the far support 

474 # pulls it on (+t(L)). Their sum is the weight, pointing up. 

475 apex_force = (-tension[0], -tension[1]) 

476 end_force = marched[-1].end_tension 

477 return ChainSolution( 

478 apex=apex, 

479 end_point=marched[-1].end_point, 

480 segments=tuple(shapes), 

481 junctions=tuple(m.end_point for m in marched[:-1]), 

482 apex_tension_vector=apex_force, 

483 end_tension_vector=end_force, 

484 apex_tension=math.hypot(*apex_force), 

485 end_tension=math.hypot(*end_force), 

486 horizontal_tension_n=h, 

487 total_weight_n=total_weight, 

488 iterations=iterations, 

489 residual=residual, 

490 )

493# -------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

494# Damped Newton on the 2-unknown shooting problem 

495# -------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

496 

497# The unknowns are non-dimensionalised as q = (ln H, V0/H): 

498# * ln H keeps H > 0 structurally — no barrier, no clamping — and makes the 

499# Jacobian scale-free across the ten decades of tension a knob scrub can 

500# visit (a taut chain's position depends on H only through ln H, so this 

501# is also the difference between a well- and an ill-conditioned solve); 

502# * V0/H is the chain's slope at the apex, an O(1) dimensionless number. 

503_Q_STEP = 1.0e-6 

504# Largest Newton step in q-space per iteration. One unit of ln H is a factor 

505# of e in tension; letting Newton leap further from a bad Jacobian is how the 

506# solve ends up in the overflow weeds. 

507_MAX_STEP = 2.0 

508_H_FLOOR_REL = 1.0e-12 

509 

510 

511class _Infeasible(Exception): 

512 """Residual could not be evaluated at this trial point.""" 

513 

514 

515def _damped_newton( 

516 residual: Callable[[np.ndarray], np.ndarray], 

517 q0: np.ndarray, 

518) -> tuple[np.ndarray, int, float]: 

519 """Damped Newton with a finite-difference Jacobian on a 2-vector. 

520 

521 Two unknowns is far too small to be worth an analytic Jacobian or a scipy 

522 dependency: three residual evaluations per iteration, a backtracking line 

523 search on ‖r‖, and a step clamp make this robust enough to survive a knob 

524 being scrubbed through configurations that briefly have no equilibrium. 

525 """ 

526 q = np.array(q0, dtype=float) 

527 try: 

528 r = residual(q) 

529 except _Infeasible as exc: 

530 raise ValueError(f"initial guess is infeasible: {exc}") from exc 

531 norm = float(np.linalg.norm(r)) 

532 

533 for it in range(1, _NEWTON_MAX_ITER + 1): 

534 if norm < _NEWTON_TOL: 

535 return q, it - 1, norm 

536 jac = np.empty((2, 2), dtype=float) 

537 for k in range(2): 

538 # Relative step: the apex slope V0/H is O(1) for a normal arm but 

539 # runs to 1e3 or more for a nearly vertical one, where a fixed 

540 # absolute step would be lost in the mantissa and hand Newton a 

541 # column of pure noise. 

542 step_k = _Q_STEP * max(1.0, abs(q[k])) 

543 qk = q.copy() 

544 qk[k] += step_k 

545 try: 

546 rk = residual(qk) 

547 except _Infeasible: 

548 qk[k] = q[k] - step_k 

549 rk = residual(qk) 

550 jac[:, k] = (r - rk) / step_k 

551 else: 

552 jac[:, k] = (rk - r) / step_k 

553 try: 

554 step = np.linalg.solve(jac, -r) 

555 except np.linalg.LinAlgError: 

556 step = -r 

557 if not np.all(np.isfinite(step)): 

558 step = -r 

559 # Clamp componentwise against each unknown's own scale, so a steep arm 

560 # (large |V0/H|) is not held to the same absolute leash as a shallow 

561 # one while ln H stays capped at a couple of e-foldings per iteration. 

562 limits = np.array([_MAX_STEP, _MAX_STEP * max(1.0, abs(q[1]))]) 

563 excess = float(np.max(np.abs(step) / limits)) 

564 if excess > 1.0: 

565 step /= excess 

566 

567 lam = 1.0 

568 for _ in range(40): 

569 trial = q + lam * step 

570 try: 

571 r_trial = residual(trial) 

572 except _Infeasible: 

573 lam *= 0.5 

574 continue 

575 n_trial = float(np.linalg.norm(r_trial)) 

576 if math.isfinite(n_trial) and n_trial < (1.0 - 1.0e-4 * lam) * norm: 

577 q, r, norm = trial, r_trial, n_trial 

578 break 

579 lam *= 0.5 

580 else: 

581 # No downhill step exists — either converged to round-off or stuck. 

582 return q, it, norm 

583 return q, _NEWTON_MAX_ITER, norm 

584

586def _shoot( 

587 residual: Callable[[np.ndarray], np.ndarray], 

588 guesses: Sequence[tuple[float, float]], 

589 what: str, 

590 hint: str, 

591) -> tuple[np.ndarray, int, float]: 

592 """Run :func:`_damped_newton` from each guess in turn; first win returns. 

593 

594 Retrying from perturbed starts (a decade either side of the physical 

595 estimate) costs a few milliseconds and turns most "no equilibrium" reports 

596 that were really "bad initial guess" into solves. 

597 """ 

598 best_norm = math.inf 

599 for guess in guesses: 

600 if not (guess[0] > 0.0) or not math.isfinite(guess[0]): 

601 continue 

602 q0 = np.array([math.log(guess[0]), guess[1] / guess[0]], dtype=float) 

603 try: 

604 q, iters, norm = _damped_newton(residual, q0) 

605 except ValueError: 

606 continue 

607 if norm < _NEWTON_TOL: 

608 return q, iters, norm 

609 best_norm = min(best_norm, norm) 

610 raise ValueError( 

611 f"no equilibrium found for {what}: the shooting solve stalled at a " 

612 f"residual of {best_norm:.3e} (target {_NEWTON_TOL:.0e}). {hint}" 

613 )

617# Rig closure 1 — tensioned halyard (massless rope of specified tension) 

618# -------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

619 

620 

621def solve_tensioned( 

622 apex: tuple[float, float], 

623 wire_length_m: float, 

624 wire_weight_n_per_m: float, 

625 stake: tuple[float, float], 

626 rope_tension_n: float, 

627 *, 

628 samples_per_segment: int = 33, 

629 kernel: MarchKernel = march_continuous, 

630 name: str = "wire", 

631) -> ChainSolution: 

632 """Model 1: a wire of fixed cut length held by a rope of specified tension. 

633 

634 The wire hangs from ``apex``; its far end is held by a *massless* straight 

635 rope running to ``stake`` and pulling with magnitude ``rope_tension_n``. 

636 The wire's end position is **not** an input — equilibrium places it, which 

637 is the whole point: ``rope_tension_n`` is the design knob and the arm's 

638 droop is the answer. 

639 

640 The closure is that the end tension vector equals the rope pull: 

641 

642 t(L) = T_rope · (stake − end) / ‖stake − end‖ 

643 

644 As ``T_rope → ∞`` the wire converges to the straight chord of length 

645 ``wire_length_m`` from ``apex`` toward ``stake`` — the taut-limit oracle 

646 against a straight inverted-vee arm. 

647 

648 Raises :class:`ValueError` when no equilibrium exists. Two cases are 

649 physical, not numerical: a stake that is not horizontally beyond the apex 

650 (the rope would have to pull backwards, needing ``H ≤ 0``), and a stake 

651 closer to the apex than the wire is long — the wire's end runs past the 

652 stake, so again the rope reverses, and no tension rescues it. 

653 

654 The tension itself is otherwise unconstrained: the solve is exercised from 

655 1e-6 N (a wire hanging all but vertically) to 1e12 N (a straight chord) 

656 without special-casing, which is what a scrubbed UI knob demands. 

657 """ 

658 if not (wire_length_m > 0.0): 

659 raise ValueError(f"wire length must be positive, got {wire_length_m!r} m") 

660 if not (wire_weight_n_per_m > 0.0): 

661 raise ValueError( 

662 "a weightless wire has no catenary: wire_weight_n_per_m must be " 

663 f"positive, got {wire_weight_n_per_m!r} N/m (pick a WireSpec with a " 

664 "real weight_g_per_m)" 

665 ) 

666 if not (rope_tension_n > 0.0): 

667 raise ValueError( 

668 f"rope tension must be positive, got {rope_tension_n!r} N; a slack " 

669 "rope holds nothing and leaves no equilibrium for the wire's end" 

670 ) 

671 

672 span_x = stake[0] - apex[0] 

673 if span_x <= 0.0: 

674 raise ValueError( 

675 f"stake must be horizontally beyond the apex: apex x = {apex[0]!r} m, " 

676 f"stake x = {stake[0]!r} m. With the stake level with or behind the " 

677 "apex the rope would pull the wire's end backwards, which needs a " 

678 "non-positive horizontal tension (a vertical hanging chain)" 

679 ) 

680 

681 segments = ( 

682 ChainSegment( 

683 length_m=wire_length_m, weight_n_per_m=wire_weight_n_per_m, name=name 

684 ), 

685 ) 

686 total_weight = wire_length_m * wire_weight_n_per_m 

687 # Non-dimensionalise the force residual by whichever of the rope pull and 

688 # the wire's own weight actually sets the scale. Dividing by the rope 

689 # tension alone looks natural but blows the residual up (and stalls Newton 

690 # on round-off) when a knob scrub drives the tension far below the weight 

691 # the wire is asking the rope to help carry. 

692 force_scale = max(rope_tension_n, total_weight) 

693 h_floor = _H_FLOOR_REL * force_scale 

694 

695 def residual(q: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: 

696 h = math.exp(q[0]) 

697 if not math.isfinite(h) or h <= h_floor: 

698 raise _Infeasible("horizontal tension collapsed to zero") 

699 v0 = q[1] * h 

700 marched = kernel(apex, (h, v0), segments, 2) 

701 end = marched[-1].end_point 

702 rx = stake[0] - end[0] 

703 rz = stake[1] - end[1] 

704 dist = math.hypot(rx, rz) 

705 if dist == 0.0: 

706 raise _Infeasible("wire end coincides with the stake") 

707 pull = (rope_tension_n * rx / dist, rope_tension_n * rz / dist) 

708 end_t = marched[-1].end_tension 

709 return np.array( 

710 [ 

711 (end_t[0] - pull[0]) / force_scale, 

712 (end_t[1] - pull[1]) / force_scale, 

713 ], 

714 dtype=float, 

715 ) 

716 

717 # Physical first guess: pretend the wire is already straight from the apex 

718 # toward the stake, then read the tension the rope would have to apply 

719 # there. That is exact in the taut limit and merely close otherwise. 

720 ax, az = apex 

721 sx, sz = stake 

722 reach = math.hypot(sx - ax, sz - az) 

723 ux, uz = (sx - ax) / reach, (sz - az) / reach 

724 guess_h = rope_tension_n * ux 

725 guess_v1 = rope_tension_n * uz 

726 guess_v0 = guess_v1 - total_weight 

727 guesses = [ 

728 (guess_h, guess_v0), 

729 (max(guess_h, total_weight), guess_v0), 

730 (0.1 * max(guess_h, total_weight), guess_v0), 

731 (10.0 * max(guess_h, total_weight), guess_v0), 

732 (total_weight, -total_weight), 

733 ] 

734 hint = ( 

735 f"With a rope tension of {rope_tension_n:g} N pulling a {wire_length_m:g} m " 

736 f"wire toward a stake {reach:g} m away, check that the stake is farther " 

737 "from the apex than the wire is long (otherwise the taut wire overshoots " 

738 "it and the rope must pull backwards) and that the tension is large " 

739 "enough to keep the wire off the vertical." 

740 ) 

741 q, iters, norm = _shoot( 

742 residual, guesses, "the tensioned-halyard rig (model 1)", hint 

743 ) 

744 

745 h = math.exp(q[0]) 

746 v0 = q[1] * h 

747 marched = kernel(apex, (h, v0), segments, samples_per_segment) 

748 return _assemble(apex, (h, v0), segments, marched, iters, norm)

752# Rig closure 2 — heavy rope of fixed length to a ground anchor 

753# -------------------------------------------------------------------------- 

754 

755 

756def solve_anchored( 

757 apex: tuple[float, float], 

758 segments: Sequence[ChainSegment], 

759 anchor: tuple[float, float], 

760 *, 

761 samples_per_segment: int = 33, 

762 kernel: MarchKernel = march_continuous, 

763) -> ChainSolution: 

764 """Model 2: a multi-segment chain of fixed lengths landing on an anchor. 

765 

766 The chain — typically antenna wire then halyard, each with its own length 

767 and weight per metre — hangs from ``apex`` and its far end must land on 

768 ``anchor``. Fixed endpoints plus inextensible fixed lengths leave **no 

769 tension freedom**: tension is an *output* here, a readout, not a knob. The 

770 knob a winch actually turns is the rope's take-up, i.e. its ``length_m``. 

771 

772 The closure is simply ``end == anchor``; the two unknowns are again 

773 ``(H, V0)``. 

774 

775 Raises :class:`ValueError` when the chain cannot reach — the straight-line 

776 distance to the anchor exceeds the total arc length (a rope shorter than 

777 the gap) — or when the anchor is not horizontally beyond the apex, which 

778 would need a vertical hanging chain. 

779 """ 

780 segments = tuple(segments) 

781 if not segments: 

782 raise ValueError("a chain needs at least one segment") 

783 

784 total_length = sum(s.length_m for s in segments) 

785 total_weight = sum(s.length_m * s.weight_n_per_m for s in segments) 

786 if total_weight <= 0.0: 

787 raise ValueError( 

788 "a weightless chain has no catenary: every segment has " 

789 "weight_n_per_m = 0, so the shape is a straight line and no " 

790 "tension can be recovered (pick a WireSpec with a real " 

791 "weight_g_per_m)" 

792 ) 

793 

794 dx = anchor[0] - apex[0] 

795 dz = anchor[1] - apex[1] 

796 reach = math.hypot(dx, dz) 

797 if reach >= total_length: 

798 raise ValueError( 

799 f"chain cannot reach the anchor: the straight-line distance is " 

800 f"{reach:.6g} m but the segments total only {total_length:.6g} m of " 

801 "arc. Lengthen the rope (reduce the take-up) or move the anchor " 

802 "closer; an inextensible chain has no equilibrium once it is pulled " 

803 "straight." 

804 ) 

805 if dx <= 0.0: 

806 raise ValueError( 

807 f"anchor must be horizontally beyond the apex: apex x = {apex[0]!r} m, " 

808 f"anchor x = {anchor[0]!r} m. A chain hanging straight down needs " 

809 "zero horizontal tension, a degenerate equilibrium this catenary " 

810 "formulation cannot represent" 

811 ) 

812 

813 h_floor = _H_FLOOR_REL * total_weight 

814 

815 def residual(q: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: 

816 h = math.exp(q[0]) 

817 if not math.isfinite(h) or h <= h_floor: 

818 raise _Infeasible("horizontal tension collapsed to zero") 

819 v0 = q[1] * h 

820 marched = kernel(apex, (h, v0), segments, 2) 

821 end = marched[-1].end_point 

822 return np.array( 

823 [ 

824 (end[0] - anchor[0]) / total_length, 

825 (end[1] - anchor[1]) / total_length, 

826 ], 

827 dtype=float, 

828 ) 

829 

830 # Straight-chord initialisation via the shallow-parabola relations: a 

831 # parabola of sag f over a chord of length C carries an excess arc length 

832 # of 8f²/(3C), and its sag is w·C²/(8·H). Invert both to get an H that 

833 # already has the right order of magnitude, then offset the apex slope by 

834 # the parabola's end slope, 4f/C. 

835 slack = total_length - reach 

836 sag_guess = math.sqrt(max(3.0 * reach * slack / 8.0, 1e-30)) 

837 w_mean = total_weight / total_length 

838 chord_slope = dz / dx 

839 guess_h = max(w_mean * dx * reach / (8.0 * sag_guess), 1e-9 * total_weight) 

840 guess_v0 = guess_h * (chord_slope - 4.0 * sag_guess / reach) 

841 guesses = [ 

842 (guess_h, guess_v0), 

843 (guess_h, guess_h * chord_slope), 

844 (0.1 * guess_h, guess_v0), 

845 (10.0 * guess_h, guess_v0), 

846 (total_weight, -total_weight), 

847 ] 

848 hint = ( 

849 f"The chain totals {total_length:.6g} m of arc across a {reach:.6g} m gap " 

850 f"(slack {slack:.6g} m); very small slack makes the tension diverge and " 

851 "the solve ill-conditioned — lengthen the rope slightly." 

852 ) 

853 q, iters, norm = _shoot(residual, guesses, "the anchored-chain rig (model 2)", hint) 

854 

855 h = math.exp(q[0]) 

856 v0 = q[1] * h 

857 marched = kernel(apex, (h, v0), segments, samples_per_segment) 

858 return _assemble(apex, (h, v0), segments, marched, iters, norm)

861def solve_sprung( 

862 apex: tuple[float, float], 

863 segments: Sequence[ChainSegment], 

864 anchor: tuple[float, float], 

865 spring_l0_m: float, 

866 spring_k_n_per_m: float, 

867 *, 

868 samples_per_segment: int = 33, 

869 kernel: MarchKernel = march_continuous, 

870) -> ChainSolution: 

871 """Model 3: a chain whose far end reaches the anchor through a bungee. 

872 

873 The chain hangs from ``apex`` as in the other two closures; its far end is 

874 neither tied straight to ``anchor`` (:func:`solve_anchored`) nor pulled at 

875 a caller-specified tension (:func:`solve_tensioned`). Instead it is 

876 coupled to the anchor through a *massless* linear spring of natural length 

877 ``spring_l0_m`` and stiffness ``spring_k_n_per_m``: being massless, the 

878 spring is straight and transmits the chain's own end tension unchanged, so 

879 it stretches to exactly whatever length that tension demands, 

880 

881 L_s = spring_l0_m + T_end / spring_k_n_per_m, T_end = |t(L)| 

882 

883 and the closure is that the chain's end, walked forward by ``L_s`` along 

884 the tension direction, lands on the anchor: 

885 

886 end + L_s · t̂_end == anchor, t̂_end = t(L) / T_end 

887 

888 The unknowns are again ``(H, V0)``. A spring end can only pull — 

889 ``T_end`` is always positive and ``L_s`` is always at least 

890 ``spring_l0_m``, never less, so there is no "pushing" branch to guard 

891 against. The only way this closure can fail to have a solution is 

892 geometric: no tension state closes the gap to the anchor, which the 

893 shooting solve reports as the module's usual "no equilibrium found" error. 

894 

895 ``end_point`` on the returned :class:`ChainSolution` is the *chain's* end 

896 — where the spring attaches, not the anchor — and ``end_tension_vector`` 

897 is the spring's pull on the chain, the same convention as the other two 

898 closures. The spring's own stretched geometry needs no extra fields to 

899 recover: its stretched length is ``hypot(anchor − end_point)`` (that is 

900 the closure itself) and its stretch beyond natural length is 

901 ``end_tension / spring_k_n_per_m``. 

902 

903 Two limits recover the existing closures and are this one's oracles: 

904 

905 * ``spring_k_n_per_m → ∞`` is :func:`solve_anchored` to a rigid link of 

906 length ``spring_l0_m``; taking ``spring_l0_m = 0`` shrinks that link 

907 to nothing, so the whole solution — ``H``, ``V0``, both tensions, the 

908 shape — converges to ``solve_anchored`` with the *same* anchor. 

909 * ``spring_k_n_per_m → 0`` degenerates to :func:`solve_tensioned`: the 

910 spring becomes so soft that its tension is set almost entirely by 

911 geometry (``T_end ≈ spring_k_n_per_m · (reach − arc − l0)``), which is 

912 exactly "how far you stretched the cord" — the same knob 

913 :func:`solve_tensioned` takes as an explicit input. 

914 

915 Raises :class:`ValueError` for ``spring_k_n_per_m ≤ 0``, ``spring_l0_m < 

916 0``, an empty or weightless chain, an anchor not horizontally beyond the 

917 apex (same degenerate-vertical-chain rationale as the other two 

918 closures), or a geometry the shooting solve cannot close — whose hint 

919 calls out the spring's stiffness and natural length as the first things 

920 to check. 

921 """ 

922 segments = tuple(segments) 

923 if not segments: 

924 raise ValueError("a chain needs at least one segment") 

925 if not (spring_k_n_per_m > 0.0): 

926 raise ValueError( 

927 f"spring stiffness must be positive, got {spring_k_n_per_m!r} N/m; " 

928 "a non-positive stiffness is not a spring (zero transmits no " 

929 "force, negative pushes)" 

930 ) 

931 if spring_l0_m < 0.0: 

932 raise ValueError( 

933 f"spring natural length must be non-negative, got {spring_l0_m!r} m" 

934 ) 

935 

936 total_length = sum(s.length_m for s in segments) 

937 total_weight = sum(s.length_m * s.weight_n_per_m for s in segments) 

938 if total_weight <= 0.0: 

939 raise ValueError( 

940 "a weightless chain has no catenary: every segment has " 

941 "weight_n_per_m = 0, so the shape is a straight line and no " 

942 "tension can be recovered (pick a WireSpec with a real " 

943 "weight_g_per_m)" 

944 ) 

945 

946 dx = anchor[0] - apex[0] 

947 dz = anchor[1] - apex[1] 

948 reach = math.hypot(dx, dz) 

949 if dx <= 0.0: 

950 raise ValueError( 

951 f"anchor must be horizontally beyond the apex: apex x = {apex[0]!r} m, " 

952 f"anchor x = {anchor[0]!r} m. A chain hanging straight down needs " 

953 "zero horizontal tension, a degenerate equilibrium this catenary " 

954 "formulation cannot represent" 

955 ) 

956 

957 # Non-dimensionalise positions by the chain's own arc length plus the 

958 # spring's unstretched length: the same "length that actually sets the 

959 # geometry" idea as solve_anchored's total_length, extended to cover a 

960 # rig where a long soft cord, not the short chain, dominates the scale. 

961 scale = total_length + spring_l0_m 

962 h_floor = _H_FLOOR_REL * total_weight 

963 

964 def residual(q: np.ndarray) -> np.ndarray: 

965 h = math.exp(q[0]) 

966 if not math.isfinite(h) or h <= h_floor: 

967 raise _Infeasible("horizontal tension collapsed to zero") 

968 v0 = q[1] * h 

969 marched = kernel(apex, (h, v0), segments, 2) 

970 end = marched[-1].end_point 

971 end_t = marched[-1].end_tension 

972 t_end = math.hypot(*end_t) 

973 ux, uz = end_t[0] / t_end, end_t[1] / t_end 

974 stretch = spring_l0_m + t_end / spring_k_n_per_m 

975 return np.array( 

976 [ 

977 (anchor[0] - (end[0] + stretch * ux)) / scale, 

978 (anchor[1] - (end[1] + stretch * uz)) / scale, 

979 ], 

980 dtype=float, 

981 ) 

982 

983 # Two limiting guesses bracket the physical range: 

984 # 

985 # (a) the stiff-spring limit: the spring barely stretches beyond its 

986 # natural length, so pretend it is a rigid link of length 

987 # spring_l0_m and reuse solve_anchored's own straight-chord/parabola 

988 # guess, aimed at the pseudo-anchor that rigid link would attach to 

989 # — the real anchor pulled back toward the apex by spring_l0_m along 

990 # the apex-anchor line. 

991 # (b) the soft-spring limit: the spring supplies essentially all the 

992 # compliance, so estimate its tension from how far the chain's own 

993 # arc length falls short of the total reach, then guess the state a 

994 # massless rope at that tension would produce (solve_tensioned's own 

995 # guess), pulling along the apex-anchor line. 

996 ux_chord, uz_chord = dx / reach, dz / reach 

997 guesses: list[tuple[float, float]] = [] 

998 

999 px = anchor[0] - spring_l0_m * ux_chord 

1000 pz = anchor[1] - spring_l0_m * uz_chord 

1001 pdx = px - apex[0] 

1002 pdz = pz - apex[1] 

1003 preach = math.hypot(pdx, pdz) 

1004 if pdx > 0.0 and 0.0 < preach < total_length: 

1005 slack = total_length - preach 

1006 sag_guess = math.sqrt(max(3.0 * preach * slack / 8.0, 1e-30)) 

1007 w_mean = total_weight / total_length 

1008 chord_slope = pdz / pdx 

1009 guess_h_a = max(w_mean * pdx * preach / (8.0 * sag_guess), 1e-9 * total_weight) 

1010 guess_v0_a = guess_h_a * (chord_slope - 4.0 * sag_guess / preach) 

1011 guesses.append((guess_h_a, guess_v0_a)) 

1012 guesses.append((guess_h_a, guess_h_a * chord_slope)) 

1013 

1014 tension_guess = spring_k_n_per_m * max( 

1015 reach - total_length - spring_l0_m, 1.0e-6 * max(total_weight, 1.0) 

1016 ) 

1017 guess_h_b = tension_guess * ux_chord 

1018 guess_v1_b = tension_guess * uz_chord 

1019 guess_v0_b = guess_v1_b - total_weight 

1020 guesses.append((guess_h_b, guess_v0_b)) 

1021 guesses.append((0.1 * max(guess_h_b, total_weight), guess_v0_b)) 

1022 guesses.append((10.0 * max(guess_h_b, total_weight), guess_v0_b)) 

1023 guesses.append((total_weight, -total_weight)) 

1024 

1025 hint = ( 

1026 f"With a spring of natural length {spring_l0_m:g} m and stiffness " 

1027 f"{spring_k_n_per_m:g} N/m coupling a {total_length:.6g} m chain to an " 

1028 f"anchor {reach:.6g} m away, check that the stiffness and natural " 

1029 "length are not so small together that no tension state closes the " 

1030 "gap, and that the anchor is far enough beyond the apex to keep the " 

1031 "chain off the vertical." 

1032 ) 

1033 q, iters, norm = _shoot(residual, guesses, "the sprung-chain rig (model 3)", hint) 

1034 

1035 h = math.exp(q[0]) 

1036 v0 = q[1] * h 

1037 marched = kernel(apex, (h, v0), segments, samples_per_segment) 

1038 return _assemble(apex, (h, v0), segments, marched, iters, norm)
