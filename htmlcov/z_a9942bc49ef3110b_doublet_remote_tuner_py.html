1"""Centre-fed doublet on window line, through a balun into a REMOTE L-NETWORK 

2TUNER — WA7ARK's station on QRZ. 

3 

4The station: a flat doublet `length_ft` long, `height_ft` up, fed at its 

5centre with `line_ft` of window line that comes down to a balun at a remote 

6tuner, whose 50 Ω side goes to the rig on coax (not modelled: the coax sees 

750 Ω whenever the tuner matches). Every length is in FEET, the user's units. 

8Signal path, from the rig: 

9 

10 rig (50 Ω) → L-network tuner → balun 1:1 or 1:4 

11 → BalancedLine (window line) → doublet centre gap 

12 

13The tuner is an L network you set: a series coil ``tuner_l_uH``, a shunt 

14capacitor ``tuner_c_pF``, and ``tuner_c_side``, which side the capacitor 

15sits on — across the balun ("balun", for a load resistance above 50 Ω, 

16stepped down) or across the rig ("rig", for one below 50 Ω, stepped up), 

17the choice a switched-L auto-tuner's relay makes. The two parts range over 

18the MFJ-926B remote auto-tuner's published nominal ranges, 0–24.86 µH and 

190–3961 pF (manual, "Specifications"), so whatever you or the optimizer set 

20is a setting that tuner can reach. The stock setting matches the stock 

21station at 7.15 MHz (SWR 1.005 on bs2, momwire's B-spline solver). 

22 

23The workflow, as with a manual tuner: 

24 

25- Pick the measurement frequency and run Optimize there (objective SWR, 

26 vary ``tuner_l_uH`` and ``tuner_c_pF``; the bands default to the 

27 measurement frequency). Optimize cannot flip ``tuner_c_side``: if the run 

28 ends far from SWR 1, the capacitor is on the wrong side for this load — 

29 switch it and run again. On the stock antenna the capacitor goes across 

30 the balun on 80, 20 and 10 m and across the rig on 40 m. From the 40 m 

31 setting the first run on 80 m (3.6 MHz) stops at SWR 4.9; a second run 

32 from there reaches 1.00 (on the command line, ``--mode root`` with a z0 

33 objective gets there in one). 

34- The frequency sweep then shows THAT setting's SWR across the band you are 

35 on, edge to edge: the parts are fixed, so the curve is how far you can 

36 QSY before you retune. 

37- Change band and the parts stay where they were, as on the real box; run 

38 Optimize again on the new band. 

39 

40The graphical tune, on the Smith chart, as you would do it by hand: look at 

41the load, turn the part CLOSEST to the antenna until the point lands on the 

42circle along which the other part moves it (a circle through Z0), then turn 

43the other part to slide along that circle to the centre. The measurement 

44plane ``lc`` (manual mode only) is the node between the two parts, and at it 

45the chart highlights that circle: 

46 

471. Pick the plane ``lc``. What it reads is the load with only the closest 

48 part applied. With the capacitor across the balun that part is the 

49 capacitor, and the remaining series coil moves the point along a circle of 

50 constant resistance: the chart highlights R = 50 Ω (r = 1). With it across 

51 the rig the closest part is the coil, and the remaining shunt capacitor 

52 moves the point along a circle of constant conductance: G = 20 mS (g = 1). 

53 The chart's "Y" button adds the admittance grid that circle belongs to. 

542. Turn the closest part (``tuner_c_pF`` across the balun, ``tuner_l_uH`` 

55 across the rig) until the point sits on the highlighted circle. "Sweep 

56 this knob…" in the knob's menu traces its whole locus, to see where it 

57 crosses. 

583. Pick the plane ``rig`` and turn the other part until the point reaches the 

59 centre: SWR 1. 

60 

61The picker names the planes source to antenna: "rig (tuner input)", 

62"inside tuner (L–C)" (``lc``), "tuner output / balun input" (``tuner``). 

63On 20 m (14.3 MHz), capacitor across the balun, on the workbench's default 

64ground (bs2, Sommerfeld average): 39.2 pF puts ``lc`` on the circle 

65(49.9 − j387 Ω), and 4.31 µH then reads SWR 1.04 at the rig (the coil's Q 

66leaves R at 51.8 Ω). 

67 

68From the command line, the same (``--set`` holds a knob that the run does 

69not vary): 

70 

71 antennaknobs optimize --builder wire.doublet_remote_tuner --bands 14.2 \ 

72 --params tuner_l_uH tuner_c_pF --set tuner_c_side=balun 

73 

74What the tuner SEES is the impedance at its antenna jack, after the balun: 

75the doublet's feed impedance carried down the line and divided by the 

76balun's ratio (``balun_ratio`` "1:4" divides it by 4). The measurement plane 

77``tuner`` reads that load as a VNA on the tuner's output jack, whichever side 

78the capacitor is on; ``lc``, between the tuner's parts, is the graphical 

79tune's (above). 

80 

81Where the power goes. The power budget (issue #299) itemizes every lossy 

82branch from the same solve: the window line (its matched loss plus the extra 

83the SWR on it costs: the line is a lossy `BalancedLine`, so the mismatch loss 

84is in the solve, not a correction), the balun, and the tuner's coil, and what 

85is left reaches the antenna — the power its port accepts, of which a real 

86ground then absorbs some (that third ledger is the far field's). The loss 

87assumptions, all knobs (hidden on the workbench, ``--set`` on the command 

88line): 

89 

90- antenna wire: ideal by default (``wire_type`` "ideal", as Mike's 

91 comparison is modelled); a catalog wire adds its ohmic loss as a row of 

92 its own, on every engine; 

93- line: ``window-450``'s matched loss, k1 0.035 / k2 0.0002 (dB per 100 ft 

94 = k1·√f + k2·f), DRY; wet window line loses several times that; 

95- tuner coil Q 200 (``tuner_ql``), as the other tuner designs here; the 

96 capacitors ideal (``tuner_qc`` 0: relay-switched fixed capacitors, Q in 

97 the thousands); 

98- balun: a CURRENT balun, whose windings are a short transmission line that 

99 the differential current does not flux the core through, so its loss is 

100 winding resistance, 0.3 Ω referred to the line side (``balun_r_ohm``: 

101 about 3 m of #14 copper at the 7 MHz skin depth, held constant over the 

102 bands); no magnetizing branch (``balun_lmag_uH`` 0). Give it one, with 

103 ``balun_qlmag`` for its core loss, to model a voltage balun. 

104 

105Mesh: the geometry is in feet, so the design has no design frequency (the 

106workbench shows no band row). The wire meshes at the density of the top of 

10710 m, 29.7 MHz (``MESH_MHZ``), on every band, so a band change never 

108remeshes and every band solves on a mesh fine enough for the highest. 

109 

110Band by band: what would an AUTO-tuner do? Mike's question is whether a 

111remote auto-tuner at the bottom of this line, with this balun, matches every 

112band, and where the power goes. The hidden knob ``tuner_mode`` "auto" puts 

113the tuner in its auto mode: at the frequency it is solved at it picks its 

114own parts within ``tuner_l_max_uH`` / ``tuner_c_max_pF`` (the same MFJ-926B 

115ranges; `station.l_network_tuner(tune_to=50, shunt_at="auto")`, AK#1646), 

116capacitor side included. A band whose exact match needs more than those 

117ranges is FLAGGED: the tuner tunes for the lowest SWR its parts reach, says 

118so in an advisory, and the SWR at the rig is no longer 1. (That manual also 

119states a 6–1600 Ω matching range; the parts range is the physics of it, so 

120the parts are what is checked.) 

121 

122The design's analysis ``tuner per band`` solves at each of Mike's 

123frequencies in the auto mode, for both balun ratios, and its Table prints, 

124per frequency, the load at the tuner, the coil and capacitor the auto-tuner 

125picks and the side the capacitor sits on, the SWR at the rig, each share of 

126the power the 50 Ω rig makes AVAILABLE (reflected, each loss, reaching the 

127antenna; a row adds to 100 %), and a "!!" line for any band it cannot match, 

128beside the impedance at the rig and at the tuner's jack: 

129 

130 antennaknobs analyze --builder wire.doublet_remote_tuner --analysis "tuner per band" 

131 

132The same per-band table at any knob setting (``band-loss`` puts the tuner in 

133its auto mode, the design's ``band_loss_params``): 

134 

135 antennaknobs band-loss --builder wire.doublet_remote_tuner 

136 

137Both are over Sommerfeld average ground (13 / 0.005 S/m, the command line's 

138default ground). 

139 

140Mike's example is the stock design: 130 ft at 30 ft, 30 ft of Wireman #552 

141window line with his measured velocity factor 0.97 and the nominal ~400 Ω 

142he gives for it. (The catalog's ``window-450`` entry is 450 Ω, VF 0.91; #552 

143gets no catalog entry of its own because no citable source for its Z0 and 

144loss turned up, so the line's loss coefficients are ``window-450``'s, and 

145Z0 / VF are his.) What the auto-tuner picks, on bs2 (extended kernel off), 

146Sommerfeld 13 / 0.005, 1:1 balun: 

147 

148 MHz load at the tuner C across coil capacitor reaches antenna 

149 3.6 73.9 + j293.9 Ω balun 10.7 µH 317.5 pF 95.3 % 

150 7.0 27.2 − j79.8 Ω rig 2.38 µH 408.3 pF 92.1 % 

151 14.0 211.8 + j712.7 Ω balun 4.00 µH 46.4 pF 92.6 % 

152 28.0 98.1 + j186.8 Ω balun 0.803 µH 59.7 pF 94.9 % 

153 

154Mike's own EZNEC/AutoEZ table has 75.60 + j289.84 Ω at 3.6 MHz, where the two 

155agree (and so do the parts: his 10.37 µH / 323.4 pF); at 7, 14 and 28 MHz 

156his loads differ (62.75 − j26.89, 561.21 + j443.56, 195.33 − j3.91 Ω), and 

157with them his parts. Fed HIS loads, the lossless tuner arithmetic chooses his 

158parts to within 2 %, so the tuner agrees; the loads are what differ. PyNEC 

159solves this same design to within 1.2 % of these bs2 loads. None of the 

160changes tried reproduces his upper bands (line Z0 250-700 Ω and its length 

161fitted freely, wire radius 0.5-2 mm, three grounds, doublet 130-142 ft), so 

162the difference is in the antenna model (his ground, wire and any insulation 

163are not stated), not in the line or the tuner. One consequence: on 40 m the 

164load here is below 50 Ω, so even with the 1:1 balun the capacitor belongs on 

165the rig side there, where Mike's never switches. With a 1:4 balun every load 

166is a quarter of the 1:1 load, as in his table (the balun's winding 

167resistance is on its line side, so the ratio stays exact). 

168 

169Choosing the line (and the balun): with an auto-tuner every band matches, so 

170the figure of merit is the WORST band's share reaching the antenna. The 

171multi-band optimizer cannot ask that (its objectives are impedance 

172objectives, which a retuning tuner meets on every band whatever the knobs 

173do), so ``band-loss --search`` runs an outer search: every band at each line 

174length, the tuner retuned at each, a grid then a bounded refinement 

175(`antennaknobs.band_loss`). The line has to reach the tuner on the ground, 

176so the search starts at 30 ft: 

177 

178 antennaknobs band-loss --builder wire.doublet_remote_tuner \ 

179 --each balun_ratio=1:1,1:4 --search line_ft=30:100:1 

180 

181For Mike's antenna (bs2, the stock losses): with the 1:1 balun, 30 ft is 

182already the best line (worst band 25 MHz, 91.9 % reaches the antenna; longer 

183lines only add loss there); with the 1:4 balun, 30 ft gives 91.4 % (worst 

1847 MHz) and 35.2 ft gives 92.2 % (worst then 25 MHz), the best of the four. 

185The differences are under a percentage point — a tenth of a dB — which is 

186the answer: on this antenna an auto-tuner at the bottom of 30 ft of window 

187line is within a whisker of the best this line and these baluns can do. 

188 

189Caveats: 

190 

191- The balun has no magnetizing inductance by default (a current balun, see 

192 above), so the 1:4 load is exactly the 1:1 load over 4. A voltage balun's 

193 magnetizing inductance shunts the load at the low bands. 

194- With the coil's Q, a load whose resistance sits within a few ohms of 50 

195 can be out of an L network's exact reach from either side; the auto mode 

196 then tunes for best effort (SWR 1.00-something), which the table says. 

197- The line's common-mode return ``line_zcomm`` is required by the solve (the 

198 balun's secondary floats), and it does not move the result: the doublet is 

199 symmetric about its feed, so no common-mode current flows. 

200- In the auto mode the optimizer refuses a match objective at the tuner's 

201 own frequency: the tuner meets it there whatever the knobs do (AK#1664). 

202 The manual tuner has no such refusal: its parts are what Optimize varies. 

203- Engines: bs2 and PyNEC (they agree to 1.2 % on the tuner's load). 

204 NEC-5 refuses it by name: its reducer route cannot address the floating 

205 centre gap. 

206"""

273def manual_l_tuner(l_uH, c_pF, c_side, *, ql=None, qc=None) -> Composite: 

274 """The manual L network, with the node BETWEEN its parts as a formal, 

275 ``lc``, so the design can name it a measurement plane. Formals ``rig``, 

276 ``lc``, ``out``; electrically `station.l_network_tuner` with fixed parts. 

277 

278 Picking a plane drops everything on the source side and keeps a shunt 

279 AT the plane node (`plane.driven_at`), so each part sits where the cut 

280 does the right thing, and an ideal short (a `TwoPort` with no element, 

281 Z = 0) carries the node to the other end of the box: 

282 

283 - ``c_side`` "balun": rig —coil— lc (capacitor across it) —short— out. 

284 At ``lc`` the coil is gone and the capacitor stays: the load with the 

285 part closest to the antenna applied. At ``out`` the capacitor goes with 

286 the upstream, so the ``tuner`` plane is the bare load. 

287 - ``c_side`` "rig": rig (capacitor across it) —short— lc —coil— out. At 

288 ``lc`` the capacitor goes with the upstream and the coil stays. 

289 """ 

290 # The short is written toward ``lc`` on both sides, so each of the four 

291 # branches has a power-budget label of its own (``budget_labels``). 

292 coil = TwoPort(a="lc", b="out", l=l_uH * 1e-6, ql=ql) 

293 short = TwoPort(a="lc", b="rig") 

294 if c_side == "balun": 

295 coil = TwoPort(a="rig", b="lc", l=l_uH * 1e-6, ql=ql) 

296 short = TwoPort(a="out", b="lc") 

297 cap = Shunt(port="lc" if c_side == "balun" else "rig", c=c_pF * 1e-12, qc=qc) 

298 draw_l = series("inductor", f"{l_uH:g} µH") 

299 draw_c = shunt("capacitor", f"{c_pF:g} pF") 

300 return Composite( 

301 ports=("rig", "lc", "out"), 

302 branches=(coil, cap, short), 

303 # Drawn from the rig side: the shunt comes first when it sits there. 

304 schematic=(draw_l, draw_c) if c_side == "balun" else (draw_c, draw_l), 

305 )

501 # ---- geometry ------------------------------------------------------- 

502 def build_wires(self): 

503 arm = 0.5 * self.length_ft * FT 

504 z = self.height_ft * FT 

505 # The geometry is in feet, so there is no design frequency: the wire 

506 # meshes at 10 m's density (MESH_MHZ) whatever band it is used on. 

507 quarter_wave = 0.25 * self.c_light_mhz_m / MESH_MHZ 

508 n = self.segs_for(2.0 * arm, quarter_wave) 

509 # One flat wire along y; the feed is a floating gap at its centre, 

510 # both terminals exposed for the balanced line. 

511 return [Wire((0.0, -arm, z), (0.0, arm, z), n, name="feed")]

558 """The circle the Smith chart highlights at each measurement plane:
