1"""The per-engine default mesh density, as one table (antennaknobs#1543). 

2 

3``nominal_nsegs`` is a DENSITY, not a count: ``AntennaBuilder.auto_mesh`` 

4reads it as segments per quarter-wave at ``design_freq``, so one number is 

5comparable across designs. What N a solver needs to be converged is a 

6property of its BASIS, which is why the number belongs to the engine and not 

7to the design. 

8 

9This table is the only place those numbers are written down. The web roster 

10(``web/adapter.py``'s ``default_n_per_wire``) and the CLI both read it, so a 

11density quoted from the app and the same density quoted from a command line 

12cannot drift apart — before #1543 they were two tables and the CLI's was 

13empty, which is how the app's razor-2p tab came to run at 15. 

14 

15Keys are the ROSTER names. The CLI's extra spellings (``bspline-d1``, 

16``razor-nec5``) resolve to a key here rather than growing a row, because they 

17are spellings of the same engine and a second row would be a second number to 

18move. 

19 

20WHERE EACH NUMBER COMES FROM 

21 

22- ``bspline`` is per DEGREE, and only bspline is: the degree IS the basis, and 

23 the basis is what sets the density. d=2 at 15 and d=1 at 20 are the 

24 basis-convergence census (docs/status/2026-07-20) — within 2% of the 

25 basis-agreed limit on 50/66 scorable designs at d=2, with d=1 needing the 

26 larger mesh to reach the same answer. d=3 at 16 (Steve, 2026-09-16, on 

27 the ΔΓ re-cut of the served-rung census, AK#1552 / #1553): at 12 its 

28 MEDIAN matched the others (ΔΓ 0.0040 against bs2@160, beside d=2's 

29 0.0037 and d=1's 0.0076) but its TAIL did not — worst 0.90 on 

30 ``short_dipole_loaded`` with the reactance's sign wrong, nine times d=1's 

31 worst — and a served density is judged on what a user can hit, not on the 

32 median. Measured at 16 the same day (Skylake, 206 cells, record branch 

33 ``scratch/1553-d3-at-16``): median ΔΓ 0.0035, p90 0.074, worst 0.74; the 

34 one genuine density row (``continuous_helix``) improves by a third, the 

35 loaded dipole by 17 % (a conditioning case no served density rescues — 

36 judge it at the antenna port), the zepp not at all (its fed wire is one 

37 fixed segment); catalog cold total 1.41x that of 12. bs2@160 is near the 

38 floor of what it can adjudicate at this density (admissible rows fell 

39 158 to 140 as the errors shrank), so saying more needs a deeper 

40 reference. Even, so a centre-fed wire keeps a knot at its middle. 

41- ``razor-2p`` and ``nec5`` at 40: one number for the two first-order 

42 engines, so an A/B between momwire's formulation twin and the licensed 

43 binary is not also an A/B on the mesh. It is higher than bspline's because 

44 both engines converge at FIRST order in the segment count (the #1525 

45 density ladder: razor-2p's fitted order is ~0.9 against bs2 refined to 160 

46 per wire; on ΔΓ, the metric the July corpus benchmark scores on, its 

47 median at 40 is 0.0129 against bs2@160 where d=2 at 15 reads 0.0037 — 

48 AK#1553's re-cut), where bs2 is within a few percent of its limit at 15. EVEN because both want a source at a segment 

49 end / a knot at the wire's exact middle. The number itself is the #1525 

50 decision (2026-09-16). 

51- ``arrayblock``, ``pynec``, ``nec2`` at 21 — the Builder framework default 

52 (``AntennaBuilder.FRAMEWORK_PARAMS``), odd for the same feed-knot reason. 

53- ``sinusoidal`` at 21: it is NEC-2's own basis, so it meshes as ``nec2`` and 

54 ``pynec`` do and an A/B across the three is not an A/B on the mesh. 

55 ``sinusoidal-galerkin`` at 20 (both decided 2026-09-16 on #1543). 

56- ``pulse`` at 41: a pulse row IS a segment, so the basis is odd-parity like 

57 ``nec2``'s, and it wants the denser mesh of a first-order basis (decided 

58 2026-09-16 on #1543). 

59- ``hmatrix`` at 30, unchanged and unmeasured: no census has said otherwise. 

60 

61An engine ABSENT from this table has no antennaknobs opinion, and its caller 

62falls back to the Builder framework default. That is what ``--engine 

63momwire`` with no basis does, which is why a command line that names no basis 

64meshes exactly as it did before #1543. 

65""" 

66 

67from collections.abc import Mapping 

68 

69# Engine/roster name -> segments per quarter-wave at design_freq. 

70DEFAULT_NSEGS: Mapping[str, int] = { 

71 "sinusoidal": 21, 

72 "sinusoidal-galerkin": 20, 

73 "bspline": 15, 

74 "pulse": 41, 

75 "hmatrix": 30, 

76 "arrayblock": 21, 

77 "razor-2p": 40, 

78 "pynec": 21, 

79 "nec5": 40, 

80 "nec2": 21, 

81} 

82 

83# Engine name -> degree -> density, for the engines whose degree is their 

84# basis. The degree the solver itself defaults to (``_OPTION_SPECS["degree"]``, 

85# 2) must map to the same number as the flat entry above, or a swap and a 

86# degree change would disagree about the stock mesh; gated in 

87# tests/test_density_1543.py. 

88# 

89# hmatrix and arrayblock expose `degree` too and are deliberately NOT here: 

90# they are accelerators chosen for SIZE, and no census has measured a 

91# per-degree density for them, so they keep one number across the tab. 

92NSEGS_BY_DEGREE: Mapping[str, Mapping[int, int]] = { 

93 "bspline": {1: 20, 2: 15, 3: 16}, 

94} 

95 

96 

97def default_nsegs(name: str, *, degree: int | None = None) -> int | None: 

98 """This engine's default density, or None when it has no entry. 

99 

100 None means "no antennaknobs opinion" and never 0 — the caller keeps the 

101 Builder framework default. A `degree` this engine has no per-degree row 

102 for falls back to its flat entry rather than to None, so an engine that 

103 merely ACCEPTS a degree keeps one density across it. 

104 """ 

105 by_degree = NSEGS_BY_DEGREE.get(name) 

106 if by_degree is not None and degree is not None and degree in by_degree: 

107 return by_degree[degree] 

108 return DEFAULT_NSEGS.get(name) 

109 

110 

111def nsegs_by_degree(name: str) -> dict[int, int] | None: 

112 """This engine's per-degree densities as a fresh dict, or None. 

113 

114 A fresh dict because this crosses the wire (the roster payload) and into 

115 a CLI resolver; a shared mapping handed out here would let one caller 

116 edit the table for every other. 

117 """ 

118 by_degree = NSEGS_BY_DEGREE.get(name) 

119 return None if by_degree is None else dict(by_degree)
